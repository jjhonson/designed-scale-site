Selected work

Selected operating experience.

These examples come from roles at previous companies, not Designed Scale client engagements. They show some of the commercial problems I've worked through, what I contributed, and what happened next.

GitHub
Product & Developer Marketing

Growing an enterprise business without losing the developers.

GitHub had built extraordinary adoption among open-source developers before it had a substantial enterprise sales organization. The opportunity was to build an enterprise business on that foundation, without losing the community that made GitHub successful.

I joined to lead product and developer marketing as the enterprise sales organization expanded. My work included enterprise positioning and sales enablement, but also education, corporate participation in open source, and relationships with project maintainers. Those community investments were part of the commercial model, not separate from it.

GitHub's enterprise business grew considerably during that period. Contemporary reporting put enterprise annual recurring revenue at approximately $25 million in 2014 and $70 million in 2016. GitHub continued growing and was acquired by Microsoft in 2018.

Genesis
CMO → CRO

From selling development tools to selling financial applications.

Genesis had raised substantial capital to build a low-code development platform for financial institutions. But getting centralized technology organizations to adopt new developer tools was difficult. More immediate demand often came from business units that needed specific workflows their internal development teams were struggling to deliver.

I joined to lead marketing and later took responsibility for the broader go-to-market organization. We shifted our commercial emphasis toward packaged financial applications, including primary bond issuance and trade allocation. I helped define the offerings, buyers, packaging, pricing and messaging, then aligned prospecting, events, content and sales enablement with specialists in markets such as fixed income and equities.

By my final years, these tailored and licensed applications had become the principal source of new business. Our longer-term plan was to use those deployments to introduce the underlying platform to banks' development organizations, but that second phase was still at an early stage when I left.

Improbable
Gaming & developer platform · CMO → CCO

When a developer-led growth model doesn't transfer.

Improbable was building SpatialOS, a platform for more complex multiplayer games. I was hired in part because of my experience at GitHub, with the idea of combining bottom-up developer adoption and enterprise selling to major studios.

We built developer programs around open access, hackathons and community events, while supporting conversations with major game makers. The interest was real, but the economics were different. An individual developer can build a meaningful open-source project; producing a substantial multiplayer game usually requires a large team, considerable investment and years of work. Studio buyers also had good reasons to resist adding production risk to already expensive games.

Experimentation and enthusiastic advocates didn't reliably become finished games or repeatable platform sales. It was a practical lesson in why a successful go-to-market model has to fit the product's smallest useful unit of adoption, not just the audience it hopes to reach.

Improbable / Skyral
Government simulation · CMO → CCO

Giving complex simulation technology a market-facing identity.

Improbable also had a small government-simulation operation run by people with military backgrounds. Its technology could model complex scenarios and decisions, but it needed a clear product identity and an explanation of its value for an audience very different from game developers.

I led the project to establish the Skyral brand and develop the platform's value proposition and positioning, working alongside the specialist team building the technology and government relationships. Much of the work was foundational marketing: explaining what the platform did, who it was for and why its capabilities mattered.

Building a government business also required specialist leadership, institutional relationships and changes in corporate structure beyond my marketing remit. The U.K. defence operation was sold in 2023 and became an independent business under the Skyral name. That was the outcome of a much broader team effort.
